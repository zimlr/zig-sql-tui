# Writing style

Project prose must read like a technical manual or engineering standard. Use plain, declarative, impersonal language and imperative rules.

## Voice and structure

- State facts, constraints, and procedures directly without narrative framing.
- Do not use humor, sarcasm, irony, narrative pacing, or promotional language.
- Omit rhetorical questions, narrator personality, direct address, and first-person pronouns.
- Remove assistant filler such as `Great question`, `Certainly`, `Here's the thing`, `It's worth noting`, and `In conclusion`.
- Prohibit promotional terms such as `battle-tested`, `bulletproof`, `blazing`, `supercharge`, `unlock`, and `journey`.
- Avoid punchy fragments, rhythmic lists, contrastive reversals, repeated sentence openings, and paragraph-final punchlines.
- Keep most sentences between 8 and 25 words. Stacked short sentences must not create dramatic effect.
- Give each paragraph one purpose, and omit scene-setting, narrative transitions, and closing morals.
- Choose concrete technical nouns and verbs over vague adjectives and marketing adverbs.
- Write plain headings that name the section content.

## Punctuation and typography

- Do not use semicolons, em dashes, or decorative en dashes in prose.
- Omit emoji, exclamation marks, and ellipses used for effect.
- Prefer periods, commas, and colons, and use parentheses only for necessary qualification.
- Code, commands, flags, and SQL may contain punctuation required by syntax.

## Enforcement

- Treat style as a hard gate equal to a passing build.
- Audit every line for prohibited punctuation, tone, and narrative patterns.
- Quote and rewrite each violation, then repeat the audit.
- Discard drafts after repeated violations and begin again.
- Preserve third-party legal notices and license text without modification.
- Automated checks cover mechanical rules only, and manual review remains required for the rest.
