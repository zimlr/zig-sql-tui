# Writing style

Project prose must read like a technical manual or engineering standard.
Use plain, declarative, impersonal language and imperative rules.

## Voice and structure

- State facts, constraints, and procedures directly.
- Exclude humor, sarcasm, irony, narrative pacing, and promotional language.
- Exclude rhetorical questions, narrator personality, direct address, and first-person pronouns.
- Exclude assistant filler such as `Great question`, `Certainly`, `Here's the thing`, `It's worth noting`, and `In conclusion`.
- Exclude promotional terms such as `battle-tested`, `bulletproof`, `blazing`, `supercharge`, `unlock`, and `journey`.
- Avoid punchy fragments, rhythmic lists, contrastive reversals, repeated sentence openings, and paragraph-final punchlines.
- Keep most sentences between 8 and 25 words. Avoid stacked short sentences for dramatic effect.
- Give each paragraph one purpose. Exclude scene-setting, narrative transitions, and closing morals.
- Use concrete technical nouns and verbs. Avoid vague adjectives and marketing adverbs.
- Use plain headings.

## Punctuation and typography

- Do not use semicolons, em dashes, or decorative en dashes in prose.
- Do not use emoji, exclamation marks, or ellipses for effect.
- Prefer periods, commas, and colons. Use parentheses for necessary qualification.
- Code, commands, flags, and SQL may contain punctuation required by syntax.

## Enforcement

- Treat style as a hard gate equal to a passing build.
- Audit every line for prohibited punctuation, tone, and narrative patterns.
- Quote and rewrite each violation, then repeat the audit.
- Discard drafts after repeated violations and begin again.
- Preserve third-party legal notices and license text without modification.
- The automated check covers mechanical rules only. Manual review remains required.
